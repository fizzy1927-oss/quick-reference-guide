Total Flow (Air-Entrainment Devices)

Venturi masks · air-entrainment nebulizers

Step 1 · Find the air : O₂ ratio

Air : O₂ = (100 − FiO₂) ÷ (FiO₂ − 21)Use FiO₂ as a percent. The answer is the air parts for every 1 part O₂.

Example, 40%: (100 − 40) ÷ (40 − 21) = 60 ÷ 19 ≈ 3, so the ratio is 3 : 1.

Step 2 · Multiply by the O₂ flow

Total flow = O₂ flow × (air parts + O₂ parts)Add the two sides of the ratio, then multiply by the flowmeter setting.

Example, 40% at 10 L/min: (3 + 1) × 10 = 40 L/min.

Is the total flow enough?
Total flow must meet the patient’s inspiratory demand. Rule of thumb: total flow ≥ 3 × minute ventilation (some programs teach 4×). If it’s too low, the patient pulls in room air and the delivered FiO₂ drops.
Not enough flow? Fixes
Run two nebulizers in tandem, increase the O₂ flow, lower the FiO₂ (more air entrained = more total flow) if the patient can tolerate it, or switch to HFNC.
Higher FiO₂ = less total flow
As FiO₂ goes up, less air is entrained, so total flow falls. That’s why air-entrainment devices struggle to give high FiO₂ at high flow.

Working backward: FiO₂ of a mixed gas

FiO₂ = [(O₂ flow × 1.0) + (air flow × 0.21)] ÷ total flow

Example: 10 L/min O₂ + 30 L/min air → (10 + 6.3) ÷ 40 = 0.41 ≈ 40% (that’s the 3 : 1 ratio).

Cylinder Duration

How long will this tank last?

The formula

Duration (min) = (gauge pressure − safe residual) × cylinder factor ÷ flowPressure in psig · flow in L/min · divide minutes by 60 for hours
  1. Read the gauge (psig).
  2. Subtract a safe residual only if the question gives one (often 200 or 500 psi).
  3. Multiply by the cylinder factor for that size.
  4. Divide by the flow in L/min.
CylinderFactor (L/psi)Full (~2,200 psi)

Where the factor comes from
Factor = cubic feet × 28.3 ÷ 2,200 psi. Oxygen cylinders are full at about 2,200 psi, and pressure drops in a straight line as the gas is used.

PISS
Pin Index Safety System — small cylinders (size E and smaller) with a yoke connector. Pins on the yoke match holes on the valve, so the wrong gas can’t be attached.
ASSS
American Standard Safety System — large cylinders (G, H/K) with threaded outlets of different sizes and thread types for each gas.
DISS
Diameter Index Safety System — low-pressure connections (under 200 psi): wall outlets, flowmeters, regulators to equipment.
Read the label, not just the color
Color codes differ between countries and can fade or be painted over. Always confirm the gas by the label before connecting. Keep cylinders upright and secured, away from heat, and never use oil or grease on fittings.
