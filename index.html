ABG Values & Oxygen Delivery

Normal adult values on room air at sea level, plus the six oxygen devices you need to know.

Normal ABG Values

Adult · room air · sea level

pH
7.35 – 7.45
< 7.35 acidosis · > 7.45 alkalosis
PaCO₂ (respiratory · acid)
35 – 45 mmHg
> 45 acidosis · < 35 alkalosis
HCO₃⁻ (metabolic · base)
22 – 26 mEq/L
< 22 acidosis · > 26 alkalosis
PaO₂
80 – 100 mmHg
< 80 hypoxemia
SaO₂
95 – 100 %
Arterial hemoglobin saturation
Base excess
−2 to +2 mEq/L
Negative = acidosis · Positive = alkalosis
ROME: Respiratory = Opposite (pH and PaCO₂ move in opposite directions) · Metabolic = Equal (pH and HCO₃⁻ move in the same direction)

Low flow
Doesn’t meet the patient’s full inspiratory demand, so room air mixes in. Faster RR or bigger VT pulls in more room air → lower FiO₂; slower, shallower breathing → higher FiO₂.
High flow
Supplies the patient’s entire inspiratory demand at a set O₂ concentration, so FiO₂ stays the same no matter how they breathe. Venturi: best for COPD / CO₂ retainers needing a precise, low FiO₂. HFNC: high FiO₂ plus a small PEEP effect and dead-space washout. How to compute total flow →

Ranges vary slightly between textbooks and facilities — follow your program’s and facility’s references.
